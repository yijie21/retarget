Method comparison

Three ways to retarget a hand GeoRT vs. TopoRetarget vs. SPIDER — and how to combine them so a robot hand can reproduce a physics-correct human–object interaction seen in video.

All three solve "human hand → robot hand," but they care about different things. Think of them as a spectrum: from copy the motion fast → copy the contact with the object → make the physics actually work.

GeoRT

Preserves fingertip motion (direction, range, smoothness, pinch). No object. Real-time.

TopoRetarget

Preserves hand–object contact structure. Geometrically feasible (no penetration).

SPIDER

Preserves dynamic feasibility — forces, contact sequences, real physics in sim.

2. Each method in its own words

Method 1 · arXiv 2503.07541

GeoRT — copy the motion, instantly

Idea
Train a tiny per-finger neural network so the robot fingertip mimics your fingertip, using 5 geometric rules: cover the full workspace, move in the same direction, respond smoothly, match pinches, avoid self-collision.
Strength
Blazing fast (1 kHz), unsupervised (no labeled hand pairs), very few knobs.
Limit for you
It has no concept of the object. It matches fingertips in free space — it won't guarantee the robot actually grips the mug or that forces are right.
Method 2 · arXiv 2606.16272

TopoRetarget — copy the contact with the object

Idea
Build a sparse interaction graph linking hand keypoints and object keypoints, then deform the robot pose so the same contacts are preserved — while penalizing penetration and respecting the robot's joint limits.
Strength
Keeps the task-relevant interaction intact (the finger that pressed the pen still presses the pen). One parameter set generalizes across robot hands; strong RL results.
Limit for you
It enforces geometric correctness (contacts, no interpenetration) but not the full dynamics — it doesn't simulate whether forces/inertia make the grasp actually hold.
Method 3 · arXiv 2511.09484

SPIDER — make the physics actually work

Idea
Take the human's kinematic motion as a "sketch," then use large-scale physics simulation with curriculum virtual-contact guidance to push it into a trajectory the robot can actually execute — correct contact timing, forces, dynamic feasibility.
Strength
Output is physics-correct and deployable. Scales across 9 embodiments / 6 datasets, producing a 2.4M-frame dataset; much faster than pure RL.
Limit for you
Offline and heavier; it refines an existing motion rather than perceiving one — it still needs the human motion handed to it.

3. Your goal: video of human–object interaction → physics-correct robot

⚠️ Reality check: none of the three takes raw video directly. They all assume the hand (and, for the latter two, the object) has already been turned into keypoints/poses. So the "visual input" part is a perception stage you add in front — none of these papers solves it.

Because they sit at different points on the spectrum, the right move is to chain them, not pick one. A pipeline that matches your aim:

STAGE 0 · PERCEPTION (not in these papers)
Video → hand + object pose Hand-object reconstruction from video (e.g. HaMeR/MANO for the hand, 6-DoF pose or mesh for the object). This produces the keypoints the methods below consume.
→
STAGE 1 · INTERACTION
TopoRetarget Map the human hand–object contacts onto the robot hand, preserving which contacts happen and avoiding penetration. Gives a geometrically faithful robot trajectory.
→
STAGE 2 · PHYSICS
SPIDER Push that trajectory through physics simulation so the grasp/manipulation is dynamically feasible — correct forces and contact timing. This is what makes it "physics-correct."
→
STAGE 3 · (optional) LIVE
GeoRT If you also want real-time teleop rather than replaying a recorded clip, GeoRT gives the 1 kHz human→robot mapping. (Object-agnostic, so it's the live driver, not the physics guarantor.)
🎯 Bottom line for your aim: the method that most directly delivers "physics-correct robot reproduction of an interaction" is SPIDER, with TopoRetarget as the natural front-end that gets the contacts right before physics refinement. GeoRT is the odd one out for this specific goal — it's the best real-time teleoperation mapper, but it ignores the object and the dynamics, so on its own it cannot guarantee a physics-correct interaction.
